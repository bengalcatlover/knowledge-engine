### Key points
*   **Cycle-to-Cycle Control:** A method of applying classical feedback control to discrete manufacturing processes to improve statistical performance by adjusting machine inputs between production cycles.
*   **Addressing Mean Shifts:** Using an integral controller guarantees that mean shifts in the process will be eliminated, driving the steady-state error to zero.
*   **The Optimal Gain:** In a discrete control system with a one-cycle delay, setting the loop gain to 1 achieves the desired target in exactly one time step.
*   **Impact on Variance (Uncorrelated Disturbances):** If the process disturbances are purely random (uncorrelated), cycle-to-cycle control will successfully center the mean but will inevitably increase the process variance.
*   **Impact on Variance (Correlated Disturbances):** If the process has "memory" or correlation (e.g., thermal inertia in an injection mold), the controller can leverage this predictability to both center the mean and significantly reduce the variance.
*   **Industry Application:** This concept is fundamentally similar to "run-by-run" or "run-to-run" control used extensively in the semiconductor industry to maintain tight process capabilities.
